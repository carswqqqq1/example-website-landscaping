Closeout and Coverage

Warranty details should be clear before the project is signed off

Think Green reviews workmanship warranty expectations before final payment so the scope, care expectations, exclusions, and follow-up process are easy to understand before the final walkthrough ends.

Before final payment, we review what is covered, what care is required, what is excluded, and who to contact if something needs attention.

What It Means

Warranty clarity is part of the closeout conversation

The goal is not a long document full of jargon. It is a simple handoff that tells you what was built, what needs normal care, and how to ask for help if something needs review.

Workmanship review

The team walks through the installed work, explains the scope that was completed, and reviews any punch-list items before closeout.

Manufacturer coverage

Products like appliances, fixtures, or specialty components may carry separate manufacturer coverage that should be kept with your records.

Maintenance guidance

The closeout notes include care reminders so plants, irrigation, lighting, and hardscape surfaces stay in good shape after completion.

Coverage Expectations

What is clarified before final payment

Warranty language should not feel mysterious. Before closeout, the team reviews the approved scope, normal care responsibilities, product documents, and how to report a concern if something needs a second look.

Written term

The exact workmanship term is named in the approved agreement and reviewed before final payment.

Workmanship scope

Installation concerns tied to the approved scope, punch-list items, hardscape setting, irrigation connections, and lighting placement are reviewed at handoff.

Plants and irrigation

Plant establishment depends on active irrigation and care. Watering schedules and controller notes are reviewed; heat stress and neglect are separate from workmanship.

Products

Manufacturer warranties for appliances, fixtures, equipment, and specialty products remain with the manufacturer, so keep product documents with your home records.

Follow-up path

Send photos and notes if something needs review. The team confirms the follow-up path and expected response timing at closeout.

Common exclusions

Normal wear, natural stone variation, weathering, owner or pet damage, neglected maintenance, and changes outside the approved scope are clarified before sign-off.

Handoff Checklist

What homeowners should leave with at the end of the job

  • A quick review of the completed scope and any remaining punch-list items.
  • Care notes for irrigation, plants, hardscape, lighting, and any installed accessories.
  • Contact details for the follow-up path if something needs attention after closeout.
  • Any equipment or product documentation that should be kept with your home records.
  • A reminder of what normal wear looks like versus something that should be reported.

Plain-Language Coverage Map

The questions we want clarified before closeout

Every project scope is different, so the final warranty summary is reviewed against the actual work performed. This map shows the categories homeowners should expect to discuss before final sign-off.

Coverage Area What Gets Reviewed Why It Matters
Workmanship Installed hardscape, fixtures, irrigation connections, lighting placement, and completed punch-list items tied to the approved scope. Helps separate a true workmanship concern from normal wear, settling, or owner maintenance.
Plants and irrigation Watering schedule, controller notes, plant establishment expectations, seasonal stress, and owner care responsibilities. Landscape performance depends on the first weeks of care, especially in Arizona heat.
Products and appliances Manufacturer paperwork for lighting fixtures, outdoor kitchen equipment, specialty accessories, and other product-specific items. Product warranties often live with the manufacturer, not the workmanship handoff.
Material exclusions Natural stone variation, weathering, owner damage, neglected maintenance, irrigation shutoff, pet wear, and site conditions outside the approved scope. Reduces surprise later by naming what normal ownership can change over time.
Follow-up path Who to contact, what photos or notes help, and how the team reviews a concern after the walkthrough. Gives homeowners a clear next step instead of wondering who owns the issue.

Common Questions

What homeowners usually want to confirm

Question Simple answer
Who do I call if something needs attention? Use the project follow-up path and the team can review the issue and point you to the next step.
Is routine maintenance part of the warranty? No. Routine care is separate, which is why the handoff includes basic maintenance notes.
Are appliances and specialty products covered the same way? Not always. Those items can have their own manufacturer coverage, so keep the product paperwork on file.
Why review all this at handoff? It removes confusion later and gives you a clean record of what was built, what was explained, and who to contact.
