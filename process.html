Project Path

What happens from first request to handoff

The goal is to make scope, budget, timing, and homeowner decisions visible before the build starts.

StepWhat HappensWhat Homeowners Get
1. Project ReviewWe review the property, goals, pain points, budget range, HOA or access constraints, and timing.Clear project-fit direction and a next-step recommendation.
2. Scope PlanningWe organize layout, materials, priority zones, utilities, phasing, and decisions that affect price.A stronger scope before money gets wasted on wrong assumptions.
3. SchedulingWe align approvals, materials, crew timing, access, and any required homeowner decisions.Fewer surprises and cleaner expectations before work starts.
4. BuildThe team executes the approved design-build scope with one accountable project path.Less fragmented handoff between design, hardscape, planting, irrigation, and lighting.
5. WalkthroughWe close out punch-list items and review care, documentation, and warranty expectations.A cleaner handoff with less ambiguity after completion.

Concierge Clarity

What we handle, and what we need from you

We handle coordination

Design-build scope, hardscape planning, planting, irrigation, lighting, material notes, crew scheduling, and closeout follow-up stay tied to one project path.

You approve decisions

Homeowners help confirm priority zones, budget comfort, materials, HOA requirements, access notes, pets, gates, and any timing constraints before work begins.

Everyone sees the same scope

Budget range, timeline, change-order conversations, and punch-list items are kept specific so the project does not drift into vague contractor promises.

HOA and Jobsite Standards

Operational details that matter before a crew arrives

For HOA-sensitive, managed, or high-access properties, the planning conversation should cover more than plants and pavers.

  • HOA packet needs, material sheets, plant palette notes, site-plan expectations, and approval timing.
  • Gate codes, parking, access windows, pets, pool access, owner or manager contact, and neighbor sensitivity.
  • Crew hours, staging area, dust and debris control, protection of existing surfaces, and daily cleanup expectations.
  • Change-order documentation, written approvals, punch-list ownership, and final walkthrough responsibilities.
  • Insurance, license, W-9, or documentation requests that need to be discussed before a site visit or board review.
